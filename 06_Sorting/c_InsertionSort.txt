================================================================================
QUESTION: Insertion Sort
FILE: c_InsertionSort.java
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS THIS PROBLEM ABOUT?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sort an array by taking one element at a time and inserting it into its correct position among the already-sorted elements to its left (like sorting playing cards in your hand).

2. HOW TO THINK (INTUITION)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hold playing cards in your hand:
Card 0 is already sorted by itself.
Pick up Card 1: if it is smaller than Card 0, slide Card 0 right and insert Card 1.
Pick up Card 2: compare with Card 1 and Card 0, shifting larger cards right until you find Card 2's spot.
Keep inserting each card into its correct position in the sorted left hand.

3. STEP-BY-STEP APPROACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Outer loop i from 0 to n - 1:
2. Inner loop j = i down to 1:
   - If arr[j - 1] > arr[j]:
     - swap(arr[j], arr[j - 1]);
   - Else: break (it's already in the right spot!).

4. WHY THIS WORKS?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At the end of iteration i, the prefix arr[0...i] is guaranteed to be sorted.

5. WHY NOT OTHER APPROACHES (WHY NOT THAT)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why not use it everywhere?
For completely reversed arrays, it does O(N^2) shifts.
However, for small arrays (N <= 16) or almost-sorted arrays, Insertion Sort is extremely fast and outperforms even Quick Sort!

6. STEP-BY-STEP DRY RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input: [12, 11, 13, 5, 6]
i = 1 (ele 11): 11 < 12 -> swap -> [11, 12, 13, 5, 6]
i = 2 (ele 13): 13 > 12 -> ok -> [11, 12, 13, 5, 6]
i = 3 (ele 5): 5 slides past 13, 12, 11 -> [5, 11, 12, 13, 6]
i = 4 (ele 6): 6 slides past 13, 12, 11 to after 5 -> [5, 6, 11, 12, 13]
Sorted!

7. COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Time Complexity: Best: O(N) (nearly sorted); Worst/Average: O(N^2)
  Reason: When sorted, inner loop breaks immediately (O(1)). When reversed, does N*(N-1)/2 comparisons.
- Space Complexity: O(1)
  Reason: In-place sorting.

8. IMPORTANT TIPS, TRAPS & EDGE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Insertion Sort is STABLE (preserves relative order of equal elements) and ADAPTIVE (runs in O(N) on sorted data).
